## First principles

Social arbitrage is information-gap investing.

1. Observe a real change in culture, consumption, technology, or policy — against *your own baseline*, not against a headline.
2. Connect it to a public company large enough for the change to be *material*.
3. Ask whether Wall Street has already written the fact into estimates, ratings, and notes (the Street Consensus Profile in `camillo_tracker.street_view`).
4. Only if the niche signal is rising *and* the fact is not clearly priced does the idea become a candidate for human work.
5. Exit as the information reaches earnings, notes, and the tape — at information parity. He has described selling 10–20% at a time. A broken thesis is also an exit, even though he does not use stop-loss orders.

Schwager titled the chapter “Neither”: neither traditional fundamentals nor technical analysis. Peter Lynch is the inspiration; Camillo drops the financial deep-dive and keeps size. The edge is being early. Tools (TikTok, Trends, TickerTags, Discord, forums) are instruments. In 2025 he exited Lululemon after concluding AI had corrupted Trends — the method is not an app.

## Decision tree

See the SVG on the site page. The gates, in order: unusual change vs baseline → 2–3 platform corroboration → materiality → priced-in test → sizing and instrument under *retail* risk rules (not his 2–30%, and not 25–30% of savings in puts) → write the thesis and the exit, then act. A “no” at any gate is a stop or a watch.
